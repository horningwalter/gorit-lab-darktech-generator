# DarkTech Taxonomy (Knowledge Base)

This file is appended to the system prompt to give the LLM concrete vocabulary for the genre.

## What is DarkTech

DarkTech is a fusion subgenre of psychedelic trance that combines:

- **Darkpsy** elements: 180-200 BPM, dark atmospheres, dissonant melodies, reversed reverb, psychedelic textures, A minor / F# minor tonalities.
- **Hitech** elements: high tempo (sometimes 200+ BPM in extreme cases), aggressive distorted kick, complex polyrhythmic percussion, glitchy tech elements, mechanical/industrial feel.

The result is darker and more aggressive than mainstream darkpsy, more atmospheric than pure hitech.

## Canonical artists (for style reference)

- **Kindzadza** (Russia): pioneer; rolling bass, acid leads, modular synth aesthetic.
- **Para Halu** (Indonesia): polyrhythmic percussion, tribal-tech fusion.
- **Hypogeo** (Italy): cinematic atmospheres, dark ambient hybrid.
- **Penta**: aggressive drops, tight production.
- **Furious**: heavy distorted basslines, acid-heavy.
- **Burn in Noise** (Brazil): organic dark psy with hitech edges.
- **Psykovsky** (Russia): forest-darkpsy roots, sparse arrangement.
- **D_A_R_K**: deep atmospheric brooding tracks.

## Sub-styles to differentiate

- **Classic DarkTech**: 185-192 BPM, balanced mix of darkpsy atmosphere and hitech aggression.
- **Hitech-dominant**: 195-205 BPM, percussion-forward, very dry.
- **Forest-tech**: organic textures, recorded sounds, slower (180-186 BPM), atmospheric.
- **Acid-DarkTech**: acid 303 lead dominant, rolling bass.

## Mandatory timbral elements

Every full DarkTech track has at minimum:
1. **Kick**: distorted, punchy, sub-heavy, 4-on-the-floor at the track BPM.
2. **Bass**: rolling (between kick hits), saturated, mono, often with FM or wavefolding.
3. **Atmosphere**: dark pad / soundscape, evolving, low-pass filtered, reversed reverb.
4. **Tech elements**: glitches, modulated noise, granular textures, percussive tech hits.
5. **Percussion**: polyrhythmic top layer (hi-hats, rim shots, metallic hits, tribal drums).

Optional but common:
6. **Acid lead** in drops (mono, resonant filter, square wave).
7. **FX** (risers, sweeps, impacts) at transitions.

## Anti-patterns to avoid

- No vocals (instrumental genre, with rare exceptions for sampled spoken word).
- No melodic chord progressions (drone/key-bound, not harmonically functional).
- No "build-drop" EDM clichés (no white-noise riser + silence + drop; DarkTech keeps momentum).
- No 4/4 disco hi-hat patterns (use 32nd-note polyrhythms or syncopation).
- No bright pads or major-key happiness.
